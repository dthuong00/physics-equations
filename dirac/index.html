Dirac asked for a quantum equation that is first-order in time, respects special relativity, and conserves probability. The answer forced spin and antimatter into the mathematics.

01

Two successful theories disagree on energy

Schrödinger uses the non-relativistic kinetic energy. Einstein says energy and momentum belong to a different geometry.

QUANTUM MECHANICS
E = p²/2m + V

Excellent when speed is much smaller than c. Its free-particle energy grows quadratically with momentum.

pE
but
SPECIAL RELATIVITY
E² = p²c² + m²c⁴

Energy has positive and negative branches. No massive excitation reaches or crosses the light speed.

pE
Taking a square root gives E = ±√(p²c² + m²c⁴). Dirac wanted an equation linear in both energy and momentum without discarding either sign.
02

Factor the relativistic energy relation

Ordinary numbers cannot make the cross-terms disappear. Dirac replaced them with matrices that anticommute.

Start with
E² = p²c² + m²c⁴
↓ seek a linear Hamiltonian
Dirac proposes
E = cα·p + βmc²
αᵢ² = β² = I

Square to identity

Each momentum and mass term reproduces the required square.

{αᵢ, αⱼ} = 0

Momentum axes anticommute

Mixed terms cancel instead of contaminating the energy relation.

{αᵢ, β} = 0

Mass anticommutes too

The momentum–mass cross-term vanishes for the same reason.

The smallest matrices that satisfy all three spatial directions are 4 × 4. Therefore ψ cannot be a single complex number: it must be a four-component spinor.
