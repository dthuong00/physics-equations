Special relativity · the full energy equation

E = mc² is only
half the story.

E²=p²c²+m²c⁴- true for everything: electrons, planets, light

The world's most famous equation is the special case for objects standing still. The full relation ties total energy to momentum and mass - and explains in one line why light can push a solar sail, why nothing massive reaches light speed, and where 99% of your own weight comes from. Walk through it, then use it to discover a particle from its debris - the way the Higgs was found.

Summary
01

What is the half that E = mc² leaves out?

A particle has energy for two separate reasons: because it moves, and because it exists. E = mc² prices the second one and says nothing at all about the first - that is the missing half. Everyday intuition says the total is simply the two added together, and that is the one thing the equation refuses to do: it adds their squares to get the square of the total. Before that can mean anything, both parts have to be in the same units, so start by regrouping the c's:

E²=(pc)²+(mc²)²
E - total energy

Everything the particle has, motion and existence combined. This is the quantity the universe actually conserves in every collision and decay.

pc - the motion part

Momentum p, converted into energy units by one factor of c. Push the particle harder and this term just keeps climbing: there is a speed limit in relativity, but there is no momentum limit.

mc² - the existence part

The rest energy - what the particle is worth when it is not moving at all. m is the invariant mass, a fixed property of the particle that no amount of speed can change.

03

Switch off one term, and two famous facts fall out

The relation has exactly two extreme cases: a particle with no momentum, and a particle with no mass. Each one is a celebrated piece of physics - and between them they account for both halves of the equation's reputation.

SET p = 0 - STAND STILL
E = mc²

The famous equation is one corner case

With the motion term gone, every scrap of E is the mass term. Einstein’s celebrity equation is the rest-frame reading of the full relation - and it says existing is expensive: one kilogram standing perfectly still stores 9×10¹⁶ J, about 21 megatons of TNT.

or
SET m = 0 - PURE LIGHT
E = pc

Massless means no choice about speed

With the mass term gone, E is nothing but pc - and that ratio has a meaning. pc/E is always the particle’s speed, as a fraction of c, because E and p both carry the same speed-dependent factor and it cancels in the ratio. Physicists write that fraction β. Here β = pc/E = 1 exactly, so anything massless must travel at c, always, in every frame - carrying momentum with no mass at all, which is why sunlight can push a solar sail.

Everything else lives between the two extremes: every electron, proton and rocket carries both terms at once. Massive things can drive the motion term as high as they like and never erase the mass term - pc grows without bound, and E stays one step ahead. But both terms are numbers somebody measured.
04

If E and p depend on who is looking, what is real?

Energy of motion depends on how fast something is going - relative to you. Two people who are themselves moving differently measure different speeds for the same particle, and write down a different E and a different p for it. Neither has made a mistake - which leaves an awkward question: does the particle have an energy at all, or only an energy according to somebody?

Everything in GeV. E changes by a factor of seven and pc climbs from nothing to almost the whole of E - both genuinely depend on who is measuring. Yet the last column never moves a digit: every observer gets the same 0.938 GeV. Mind what that number is, though - it is an energy, the proton’s mc²; the mass is 0.938 GeV/c². Physicists quote masses in GeV/c² and then leave the c² unsaid.

So the equation, rearranged, is a statement about what nobody can argue with
(mc²)²=E²−(pc)²

Which leaves one thing to explain: why should subtracting two quantities that both change leave something that does not? Because that is simply how spacetime measures distance - and you already know the version with a plus sign.

Rotate a stick

Its x and y coordinates change, but x² + y² - the length - never does. Space geometry conserves lengths with a plus sign: points slide on circles.

Boost past a particle

Its E and pc change, but E² − (pc)² never does. Spacetime geometry conserves this with a minus sign: states slide on hyperbolas instead of circles.

The analogy also hands over the object that was missing. A stick’s x and y are not the stick - they are its shadows on your axes, swinging by a fixed rule when you turn your head, while the stick is what is real and its length is what everyone agrees on. E and p are the same kind of thing: the components of one object, the four-momentum (E/c, p). E and p are its shadows; m is its length.

So mass is not “amount of stuff”. It is the invariant length of a particle’s four-momentum - the one thing about its energy and motion no observer can argue with. It comes with a catch worth knowing: to weigh two tracks in a detector you add the four-momenta first, then take the length of the sum - and the length of a sum is not the sum of the lengths. Two photons are each massless, yet together carry Mc² = √(2E₁E₂(1 − cos θ)). A system’s mass is not its parts’ masses added up - and that formula is exactly how the Higgs was found.
05

Slow down, and Newton reappears

If the relation is right, it must contain the mechanics that ran physics for two centuries. Take the square root and let momentum be small compared with mc² - the everyday case, where almost all of E is just the mass term:

A 70 kg jogger’s rest energy outweighs their kinetic energy by sixteen orders of magnitude. Everyday mechanics only ever measures energy differences, so the giant constant term stayed perfectly invisible - until nuclear reactions started showing it.

08

Almost none of your weight is “stuff”

Everyday intuition says mass is how much matter there is: a brick is heavy because it holds a lot of atoms. That picture survives right up until you weigh a proton and then weigh the three valence quarks inside it. The quarks add up to about 9 MeV. The proton is 938. Nothing is missing - the equation already told us where the rest is.

Apply the invariant to a whole system, not one particle
(Mc²)²=(ΣE)²−(Σpc)²

A system's mass is not the sum of its parts' masses. It is built from the total energy inside - motion included - minus whatever net momentum the system carries. Put energy into something in a way that leaves it nowhere to go, and the thing gets heavier. Every time.

Two photons, one mass

Fire two photons in opposite directions. Each is massless. Together, their energies add and their momenta cancel - so Σp = 0 and the pair has M = ΣE ∕ c². Mass, assembled out of two things that have none.

A hot brick is a heavy brick

Heat is just jiggling atoms - kinetic energy with no net direction. Boil a kilogram of water and you add 419 kJ, so you also add 4.7 nanograms. Real, measured in principle.

A proton is almost all internal energy

Three valence quarks in a sea of gluons and virtual quark-antiquark pairs, inside a femtometre. A resting proton has Σp = 0, so its mass is exactly the energy in there - and almost all of it is the gluon field that confines them, not the quarks.

The proton's mass budget938.3 MeV, itemised
rest mass of the 3 valence quarks≈ 9 MeV
energy of the gluon field≈ 929 MeV

The Higgs field is what gives those quarks their 9 MeV - so it accounts for roughly 1% of your body weight. The relation forces the other 99% to be internal energy: Σp = 0, so mass and energy content are the same number, and there is nowhere else for it to hide. That energy is the gluon field confining the quarks - and it is 99% of you. Your body is, almost entirely, field energy that cannot get out.

It works in both directionsadd energy, gain mass - remove it, lose mass
+A charged phone battery is about 0.6 nanograms heavier than a flat one. Chemical energy has weight. −A helium nucleus weighs 0.7% less than the two protons and two neutrons that make it: binding energy escaped as light. −The Sun runs on that difference, shedding 4 million tonnes of mass every second and shipping it out as sunlight.

Bound systems are lighter than their parts, confined ones are heavier - and both are the same equation, read with the right sign. Nuclear power stations and your own body weight are two sides of one accounting rule.

Mass was never a substance. It is the invariant length of a system's energy–momentum arrow - so anything that raises the energy inside while leaving the momentum at zero raises the mass. Einstein's own 1905 argument was exactly this, run backwards: let a body emit two flashes of light in opposite directions and it loses energy while gaining no momentum, so it must have lost mass. That four-page afterthought is where E = mc² first appears.
10

How do you weigh a particle nobody can see?

A Higgs boson is gone in 10⁻²² seconds. Nothing you can measure is its energy or its momentum - only two pieces of debris, flying apart. Its mass sounds unknowable. It is not, for the one reason this whole lesson has been about: m is invariant, so the debris still carries the answer. Four questions, then run it yourself - start with the J/ψ, where it takes seconds.

1What do you actually measure? Not the particle - two pieces of debris. For each one the detector gets a direction, plus either its momentum or its energy. 2How does it get those? A charged piece curves in a magnet: the stiffer the curve, the bigger p. A photon has no charge, so it is buried in a dense block and the light it makes gives E. The other half comes from this lesson's own relation - you know which particle it is, so you know its m. 3And that gives the parent? Add the two energies to get E, add the two momenta to get p, compute √(E² − (pc)²). Mass is invariant, so that number is the parent's mass, in GeV. 4Then why thousands of them? Nothing says which two pieces belonged together, so you do it for every pair. Wrong pairs land anywhere - the grey slope. Right pairs land on the same number every time, stacking into a bump. The bump is the particle.
