Special relativity · the full energy equation

E = mc² is only
half the story.

E²=p²c²+m²c⁴- true for everything: electrons, planets, light

The world's most famous equation is the special case for objects standing still. The full relation ties total energy to momentum and mass - and explains in one line why light can push a solar sail, why nothing massive reaches light speed, and where 99% of your own weight comes from. Walk through it, then use it to discover a particle from its debris - the way the Higgs was found.

01

Why is it Esquared, and not just E?

A moving particle carries energy for two reasons: because it moves, and because it exists. Everyday intuition says you add the two up. The equation says you do not - you add their squares, and that single difference is what the rest of this lesson is about. Start by regrouping the constants, which makes the two contributions comparable:

Group each c with its partner - every term becomes an energy, squared
E²=(pc)²+(mc²)²
E - total energy

Everything the particle has, motion and existence combined. This is the quantity the universe actually conserves in every collision and decay.

pc - the motion part

Momentum p, converted to energy units by one factor of c. It grows without limit as the particle is pushed harder - momentum has no ceiling.

mc² - the existence part

The rest energy: what the particle is worth standing still. m is the invariant mass - a fixed property of the particle that no amount of speed changes.

Notice what the c's are doing: they are unit converters, nothing more. Squares are why the bigger contribution swallows the total and the smaller one barely registers - 2 and 100 do not add to 102 here, they add to about 100. So a particle can be almost pure motion, or almost pure existence, and the same one line covers both. Next: what is left when one of them disappears completely.
03

Switch off one term, and two famous facts fall out

The relation has exactly two extreme cases: a particle with no momentum, and a particle with no mass. Each one is a celebrated piece of physics - and between them they account for both halves of the equation's reputation.

SET p = 0 - STAND STILL
E = mc²

The famous equation is one corner case

With the motion term gone, every scrap of E is the mass term. Einstein’s celebrity equation is the rest-frame reading of the full relation - and it says existing is expensive: one kilogram standing perfectly still stores 9×10¹⁶ J, about 21 megatons of TNT.

or
SET m = 0 - PURE LIGHT
E = pc

Massless means no choice about speed

With the mass term gone, E is nothing but pc, so β = pc/E = 1 exactly. Anything massless must travel at c, always, in every frame - and it carries momentum with no mass at all, which is why sunlight can push a solar sail.

Everything else lives between the two extremes: every electron, proton and rocket carries both terms at once. Massive things can drive the motion term as high as they like and never erase the mass term - pc grows without bound, and E stays one step ahead. But both terms are numbers somebody measured. Next: what happens when somebody else measures them.
04

If E and p depend on who is looking, what is real?

Energy of motion depends on how fast something is going - relative to you. So two people who are themselves moving differently measure different speeds for the same particle, and write down a different E and a different p for it. Neither of them has made a mistake. Which leaves an awkward question: does the particle have an energy at all, or only an energy according to somebody? Watch three observers measure one proton.

Everything in GeV. E changes by a factor of seven and pc climbs from nothing to almost the whole of E - the two of them genuinely are opinions. Yet the last column never moves a digit. Every observer in the universe gets that same 0.938, and that number is what we mean by the proton's mass.

So the equation, rearranged, is a statement about what nobody can argue with
(mc²)²=E²−(pc)²

Which leaves one thing to explain: why should subtracting two quantities that both change leave something that does not? Because that is simply how spacetime measures distance - and you already know the version with a plus sign.

Rotate a stick

Its x and y coordinates change, but x² + y² - the length - never does. Space geometry conserves lengths with a plus sign: points slide on circles.

Boost past a particle

Its E and pc change, but E² − (pc)² never does. Spacetime geometry conserves this with a minus sign: states slide on hyperbolas instead of circles.

So mass is not “amount of stuff”. It is the part of a particle that survives changing observer - the one number about it the whole universe agrees on. It is also the only reason anyone can weigh a particle they never see: point this same subtraction at two tracks in a detector and out comes the mass of whatever made them, which is the last slide of this lesson. Next: check the relation against the physics it had to replace.
05

Slow down, and Newton reappears

If the relation is right, it must contain the mechanics that ran physics for two centuries. Take the square root and let momentum be small compared with mc² - the everyday case, where almost all of E is just the mass term:

Exact, from the relationE = mc² · √(1 + (pc ∕ mc²)²)
Now let momentum be small√(1 + x²) ≈ 1 + x²⁄2  ⟹  E ≈ mc² + p²∕2m

A 70 kg jogger’s rest energy outweighs their kinetic energy by sixteen orders of magnitude. Everyday mechanics only ever measures energy differences, so the giant constant term stayed perfectly invisible - until nuclear reactions started cashing it in.

One relation, three regimes: Newton when the motion term is a whisper beside the mass term, E = mc² when it is gone, pure light when there is no mass term at all. Every one of those was one particle. Next: point the same relation at a bagful of them, and watch mass stop meaning “amount of stuff”.
08

Almost none of your weight is “stuff”

Everyday intuition says mass is how much matter there is: a brick is heavy because it holds a lot of atoms. That picture survives right up until you weigh a proton and then weigh the three quarks inside it. The quarks add up to about 9 MeV. The proton is 938. Nothing is missing - the equation already told us where the rest is.

Apply the invariant to a whole system, not one particle
(Mc²)²=(ΣE)²−(Σpc)²

A system's mass is not the sum of its parts' masses. It is built from the total energy inside - motion included - minus whatever net momentum the system carries. Put energy into something in a way that leaves it nowhere to go, and the thing gets heavier. Every time.

Two photons, one mass

Fire two photons in opposite directions. Each is massless. Together, their energies add and their momenta cancel - so Σp = 0 and the pair has M = ΣE ∕ c². Mass, assembled out of two things that have none.

A hot brick is a heavy brick

Heat is just jiggling atoms - kinetic energy with no net direction. Boil a kilogram of water and you add 419 kJ, so you also add 4.7 nanograms. Real, measured in principle, and far too small for any kitchen scale.

A proton is a box of motion

Three quarks moving at nearly c inside a bag a femtometre wide, glued in by a field that will not let them out. Their confined energy is the proton's mass - and therefore, near enough, yours.

The proton's mass budget938.3 MeV, itemised
rest mass of the 3 quarks≈ 9 MeV
their motion + the gluon field≈ 929 MeV

The Higgs field is what gives those quarks their 9 MeV - so it accounts for roughly 1% of your body weight. The other 99% is energy that cannot get out of a proton. You are, almost entirely, trapped motion.

It works in both directionsadd energy, gain mass - remove it, lose mass
+A charged phone battery is about 0.6 nanograms heavier than a flat one. Chemical energy has weight. −A helium nucleus weighs 0.7% less than the two protons and two neutrons that make it: binding energy escaped as light. −The Sun runs on that difference, shedding 4 million tonnes of mass every second and shipping it out as sunlight.

Bound systems are lighter than their parts, confined ones are heavier - and both are the same equation, read with the right sign. Nuclear power stations and your own body weight are two sides of one accounting rule.

Mass was never a substance. It is the invariant length of a system's energy–momentum arrow - so anything that raises the energy inside while leaving the momentum at zero raises the mass. Einstein's own 1905 argument was exactly this, run backwards: let a body emit two flashes of light in opposite directions and it loses energy while gaining no momentum, so it must have lost mass. That four-page afterthought is where E = mc² first appears. Next: point the same sum at two tracks in a detector, and weigh something nobody can see.
10

How do you weigh a particle nobody can see?

A Higgs boson is gone in 10⁻²² seconds. Nothing you can measure is its energy or its momentum - only two pieces of debris, flying apart. Its mass sounds unknowable. It is not, for the one reason this whole lesson has been about: m is invariant, so the debris still carries the answer. Four questions, then run it yourself - start with the J/ψ, where it takes seconds.

1What do you actually measure? Not the particle - two pieces of debris. For each one the detector gets a direction, plus either its momentum or its energy. 2How does it get those? A charged piece curves in a magnet: the stiffer the curve, the bigger p. A photon has no charge, so it is buried in a dense block and the light it makes gives E. The other half comes from this lesson's own relation - you know which particle it is, so you know its m. 3And that gives the parent? Add the two energies to get E, add the two momenta to get p, compute √(E² − (pc)²). Mass is invariant, so that number is the parent's mass, in GeV. 4Then why thousands of them? Nothing says which two pieces belonged together, so you do it for every pair. Wrong pairs land anywhere - the grey slope. Right pairs land on the same number every time, stacking into a bump. The bump is the particle.
