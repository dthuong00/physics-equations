Special relativity · the full energy equation

E = mc² is only
half the story.

E²=p²c²+m²c⁴— true for everything: electrons, planets, light

The world's most famous equation is the special case for objects standing still. The full relation ties total energy to momentum and mass by nothing fancier than the Pythagorean theorem — and explains in one line why light can push a solar sail, why nothing massive reaches light speed, and how the Higgs boson was found. Walk through it, then drive a particle to the edge of c yourself.

01

Three quantities, one bookkeeping law

A moving particle carries energy for two reasons: because it moves, and because it exists. The equation adds the two contributions — but not the way everyday intuition would. First, regroup the constants:

Group each c with its partner — every term becomes an energy, squared
E²=(pc)²+(mc²)²
E — total energy

Everything the particle has, motion and existence combined. This is the quantity the universe actually conserves in every collision and decay.

pc — the motion part

Momentum p, converted to energy units by one factor of c. It grows without limit as the particle is pushed harder — momentum has no ceiling.

mc² — the existence part

The rest energy: what the particle is worth standing still. m is the invariant mass — a fixed property of the particle that no amount of speed changes.

Notice what the c's are doing: they are unit converters, nothing more. Once pc and mc² are both measured in energy, the equation is free to say something geometric — squares adding to a square.
02

Pythagoras, wearing a physics costume

Two squares summing to a third is the signature of a right triangle: legs pc and mc², hypotenuse E. Here is a particle with rest energy 3 GeV carrying 4 GeV of momentum — a perfect 3–4–5 triangle.

This “3–4–5 particle” moves at v = 4/5 of light speed — the speed is literally the tilt of the hypotenuse.

How fast? β = pc ∕ E

Opposite over hypotenuse: the sine of the tilt angle. Here 4/5 = 0.8 c. Flatten the triangle and β creeps toward 1 — it can never pass it.

How stretched? γ = E ∕ mc²

Hypotenuse over the mass leg: 5/3 ≈ 1.67. This is the time-dilation factor — clocks aboard this particle tick 1.67× slower.

Motion’s share? KE = E − mc²

How much longer the hypotenuse is than the mass leg alone: 5 − 3 = 2 GeV of kinetic energy.

All of relativistic kinematics is trigonometry on this one triangle — every γ, every β, every kinetic energy is just a ratio of its sides.
03

Collapse the triangle, and two famous facts fall out

A right triangle has two degenerate cases: lose the horizontal leg, or lose the vertical one. Each collapse is a celebrated piece of physics.

SET p = 0 — STAND STILL
E = mc²

The famous equation is one corner case

With no momentum leg, the hypotenuse falls onto the mass leg. Einstein’s celebrity equation is the rest-frame reading of the full relation — and it says existing is expensive: one kilogram standing perfectly still stores 9×10¹⁶ J, about 21 megatons of TNT.

or
SET m = 0 — PURE LIGHT
E = pc

Massless means no choice about speed

With no mass leg, the hypotenuse lies flat on the momentum leg, so β = pc/E = 1 exactly. Anything massless must travel at c, always, in every frame — and it carries momentum with no mass at all, which is why sunlight can push a solar sail.

Everything else lives between the two collapses: every electron, proton and rocket is a triangle with both legs. Massive things can flatten their triangle forever without finishing the job — pc grows without bound, and E stays one step ahead.
04

Observers disagree about E and p — never about m

Chase a particle and its energy drops; run toward it and its momentum grows. E and p are opinions that depend on the observer. Rearranged, the equation names the one combination every observer computes identically:

The observer-proof combination
(mc²)²=E²−(pc)²
Rotate a stick

Its x and y coordinates change, but x² + y² — the length — never does. Space geometry conserves lengths with a plus sign: points slide on circles.

Boost past a particle

Its E and pc change, but E² − (pc)² never does. Spacetime geometry conserves this with a minus sign: states slide on hyperbolas instead of circles.

How new particles are actually discoveredthe equation used as a weighing scale
1Smash protons; measure the E and p of every piece of debris. 2For interesting combinations, compute √(E² − (pc)²) — the invariant mass. 3Histogram it. Random debris smears out; a real particle piles up at its own mass.

The Higgs boson announced itself in 2012 exactly this way — as a bump of collisions whose debris kept computing the same invariant mass, 125 GeV.

This is what mass is in relativity: not “amount of stuff”, but the invariant length of the energy–momentum arrow — the one number about a particle that the whole universe agrees on.
05

Slow down, and Newton reappears

If the relation is right, it must contain the mechanics that ran physics for two centuries. Take the square root and let the triangle be nearly vertical — momentum small compared to mc²:

Exact, from the triangleE = mc² · √(1 + (pc ∕ mc²)²)
Now let momentum be small√(1 + x²) ≈ 1 + x²⁄2  ⟹  E ≈ mc² + p²∕2m
Newton, recoveredE ≈ mc² + ½mv² — the familiar kinetic energy, riding on an enormous constant
Why nobody noticed the constant for 200 yearsa person jogging
kinetic energy ½mv²≈ 315 J
rest energy mc²≈ 6.3×10¹⁸ J

A 70 kg jogger’s rest energy outweighs their kinetic energy by sixteen orders of magnitude. Everyday mechanics only ever measures energy differences, so the giant constant term stayed perfectly invisible — until nuclear reactions started cashing it in.
