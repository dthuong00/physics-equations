Special relativity · the full energy equation

E = mc² is only
half the story.

E²=p²c²+m²c⁴- true for everything: electrons, planets, light

The world's most famous equation is the special case for objects standing still. The full relation ties total energy to momentum and mass by nothing fancier than the Pythagorean theorem - and explains in one line why light can push a solar sail, why nothing massive reaches light speed, and where 99% of your own weight comes from. Walk through it, then drive a particle to the edge of c, turn the mass shell over in your hands, weigh a box containing nothing but light, and discover a particle from its debris.

01

Three quantities, one bookkeeping law

A moving particle carries energy for two reasons: because it moves, and because it exists. The equation adds the two contributions - but not the way everyday intuition would. First, regroup the constants:

Group each c with its partner - every term becomes an energy, squared
E²=(pc)²+(mc²)²
E - total energy

Everything the particle has, motion and existence combined. This is the quantity the universe actually conserves in every collision and decay.

pc - the motion part

Momentum p, converted to energy units by one factor of c. It grows without limit as the particle is pushed harder - momentum has no ceiling.

mc² - the existence part

The rest energy: what the particle is worth standing still. m is the invariant mass - a fixed property of the particle that no amount of speed changes.

Notice what the c's are doing: they are unit converters, nothing more. Once pc and mc² are both measured in energy, the equation is free to say something geometric - squares adding to a square.
03

Collapse the triangle, and two famous facts fall out

Squares adding to a square is the signature of a right triangle - legs pc and mc², hypotenuse E - and a right triangle has two degenerate cases: lose the horizontal leg, or lose the vertical one. Each collapse is a celebrated piece of physics.

SET p = 0 - STAND STILL
E = mc²

The famous equation is one corner case

With no momentum leg, the hypotenuse falls onto the mass leg. Einstein’s celebrity equation is the rest-frame reading of the full relation - and it says existing is expensive: one kilogram standing perfectly still stores 9×10¹⁶ J, about 21 megatons of TNT.

or
SET m = 0 - PURE LIGHT
E = pc

Massless means no choice about speed

With no mass leg, the hypotenuse lies flat on the momentum leg, so β = pc/E = 1 exactly. Anything massless must travel at c, always, in every frame - and it carries momentum with no mass at all, which is why sunlight can push a solar sail.

Everything else lives between the two collapses: every electron, proton and rocket is a triangle with both legs. Massive things can flatten their triangle forever without finishing the job - pc grows without bound, and E stays one step ahead.
04

Observers disagree about E and p - never about m

Chase a particle and its energy drops; run toward it and its momentum grows. E and p are opinions that depend on the observer. Rearranged, the equation names the one combination every observer computes identically:

The observer-proof combination
(mc²)²=E²−(pc)²
Rotate a stick

Its x and y coordinates change, but x² + y² - the length - never does. Space geometry conserves lengths with a plus sign: points slide on circles.

Boost past a particle

Its E and pc change, but E² − (pc)² never does. Spacetime geometry conserves this with a minus sign: states slide on hyperbolas instead of circles.

How new particles are actually discoveredthe equation used as a weighing scale
1Smash protons; measure the E and p of every piece of debris. 2For interesting combinations, compute √(E² − (pc)²) - the invariant mass. 3Histogram it. Random debris smears out; a real particle piles up at its own mass.

The Higgs boson announced itself in 2012 exactly this way - as a bump of collisions whose debris kept computing the same invariant mass, 125 GeV.

This is what mass is in relativity: not “amount of stuff”, but the invariant length of the energy–momentum arrow - the one number about a particle that the whole universe agrees on.
05

Slow down, and Newton reappears

If the relation is right, it must contain the mechanics that ran physics for two centuries. Take the square root and let the triangle be nearly vertical - momentum small compared to mc²:

Exact, from the triangleE = mc² · √(1 + (pc ∕ mc²)²)
Now let momentum be small√(1 + x²) ≈ 1 + x²⁄2  ⟹  E ≈ mc² + p²∕2m
Newton, recoveredE ≈ mc² + ½mv² - the familiar kinetic energy, riding on an enormous constant
Why nobody noticed the constant for 200 yearsa person jogging
kinetic energy ½mv²≈ 315 J
rest energy mc²≈ 6.3×10¹⁸ J

A 70 kg jogger’s rest energy outweighs their kinetic energy by sixteen orders of magnitude. Everyday mechanics only ever measures energy differences, so the giant constant term stayed perfectly invisible - until nuclear reactions started cashing it in.

One relation, three regimes: Newton when the triangle is nearly vertical, E = mc² when it is exactly vertical, pure light when it is flat. Next: drive the triangle yourself.

One knob turns a cone into a bowl

So far momentum has been a single number, pointing along one line. Let it point anywhere in a plane instead, and plot the energy the equation demands as a height above every possible momentum. The result is a surface - physicists call it the mass shell - and a particle of that mass is allowed to sit anywhere on it and nowhere else. 08

Almost none of your weight is “stuff”

Everyday intuition says mass is how much matter there is: a brick is heavy because it holds a lot of atoms. That picture survives right up until you weigh a proton and then weigh the three quarks inside it. The quarks add up to about 9 MeV. The proton is 938. Nothing is missing - the equation already told us where the rest is.

Apply the invariant to a whole system, not one particle
(Mc²)²=(ΣE)²−(Σpc)²

A system's mass is not the sum of its parts' masses. It is built from the total energy inside - motion included - minus whatever net momentum the system carries. Put energy into something in a way that leaves it nowhere to go, and the thing gets heavier. Every time.

Two photons, one mass

Fire two photons in opposite directions. Each is massless. Together, their energies add and their momenta cancel - so Σp = 0 and the pair has M = ΣE ∕ c². Mass, assembled out of two things that have none.

A hot brick is a heavy brick

Heat is just jiggling atoms - kinetic energy with no net direction. Boil a kilogram of water and you add 419 kJ, so you also add 4.7 nanograms. Real, measured in principle, and far too small for any kitchen scale.

A proton is a box of motion

Three quarks moving at nearly c inside a bag a femtometre wide, glued in by a field that will not let them out. Their confined energy is the proton's mass - and therefore, near enough, yours.

The proton's mass budget938.3 MeV, itemised
rest mass of the 3 quarks≈ 9 MeV
their motion + the gluon field≈ 929 MeV

The Higgs field is what gives those quarks their 9 MeV - so it accounts for roughly 1% of your body weight. The other 99% is energy that cannot get out of a proton. You are, almost entirely, trapped motion.

It works in both directionsadd energy, gain mass - remove it, lose mass
+A charged phone battery is about 0.6 nanograms heavier than a flat one. Chemical energy has weight. −A helium nucleus weighs 0.7% less than the two protons and two neutrons that make it: binding energy escaped as light. −The Sun runs on that difference, shedding 4 million tonnes of mass every second and shipping it out as sunlight.

Bound systems are lighter than their parts, confined ones are heavier - and both are the same equation, read with the right sign. Nuclear power stations and your own body weight are two sides of one accounting rule.

Mass was never a substance. It is the invariant length of a system's energy–momentum arrow - so anything that raises the energy inside while leaving the momentum at zero raises the mass. Next: build one and weigh it.
09

A box of light has weight

Two photons bouncing between perfect mirrors. Nothing in the box has any mass at all - yet the box as a whole is heavier, harder to push, and would tip a scale. Push the box up to speed and watch the honest consequences: one photon blueshifts, the other reddens, the box squashes along its length, and the two bounces stop happening at the same moment - so for stretches of each cycle both photons are heading the same way and the mirrors are holding the missing momentum. Einstein, 1905His own argument was a box like this one: let a body emit two flashes of light in opposite directions, and it loses energy without gaining any momentum - so it must have lost mass. That four-page afterthought to special relativity is where E = mc² first appears.
10

Find a particle you can never see

A heavy particle made in a collision is gone long before it reaches anything: it decays in 10⁻²² seconds, leaving only debris. So you measure the debris - two tracks, each with an energy and a direction - and compute √(E² − (pc)²) for the pair. Random combinations give random answers and smear into a smooth background. But if the two really came from one parent, that number is the parent's mass every single time. Collect enough events and it stops being a number and becomes a bump.
