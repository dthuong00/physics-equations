General relativity · gravity as geometry

Gravity is not a force.
It's the shape of spacetime.

Gμν+Λgμν=(8πG∕c⁴) Tμν- ten equations that replaced Newton's one

In November 1915 Einstein replaced gravity-the-force with a single idea: mass and energy bend the geometry of space and time, and everything else simply coasts through the bends. This one line predicts falling apples, drifting GPS clocks, black holes, gravitational waves and the expanding universe. Walk through what each symbol says - then pour mass into spacetime and bend it yourself.

01

Two sides of a cosmic conversation

The left side is pure geometry - how spacetime curves. The right side is pure content - what's in it. The equals sign forces them to agree, everywhere, at every instant. The indices μ and ν each run over the four directions of spacetime {t, x, y, z}:

One tensor equation - a 4 × 4 grid of statements, one per pair of spacetime directions
Gμν+Λgμν=8πG∕c⁴·Tμνcurvature = content, at every point in the universe
Gμν - the curvature side

The Einstein tensor: a distilled summary of how spacetime bends at a point - how free-falling paths converge, volumes shrink, and clocks fall out of step. It is built entirely from the metric gμν, the field that says how much time ticks and rulers stretch. The metric is the gravitational field.

Λgμν - the vacuum's own gravity

The cosmological constant: empty space carries a whisper of energy, and it gravitates - repulsively. Einstein inserted Λ in 1917, later called it his greatest blunder, and in 1998 supernova surveys resurrected it as dark energy: today it makes up roughly 68% of the universe.

Tμν - everything in it

The stress–energy tensor: a complete inventory of energy density, momentum, pressure and stress at each point. This is what sources gravity - not mass alone. Mass is just the densest entry in the ledger.

Ten equations in one

The 4×4 grid is symmetric (μν = νμ), so 16 slots hold 10 independent equations - coupled, nonlinear partial differential equations for the metric. Newton needed one line; spacetime needs ten.

8πG∕c⁴ ≈ 2×10⁻⁴³

The exchange rate from energy to curvature is absurdly small - c⁴ in the denominator sees to that. Spacetime is the stiffest material there is: it takes a star to dent it visibly.

It feeds on itself

Curvature carries energy, and energy curves: gravity gravitates. That self-feeding nonlinearity is why exact solutions are rare treasures, and why merging black holes ring spacetime like a struck bell.

John Wheeler compressed all ten into one sentence: "Spacetime tells matter how to move; matter tells spacetime how to curve." Read the equals sign as a two-way conversation - the right side sculpts the left, and matter then coasts along the sculpture.
02

The part that pulls the apple is time

Everyone has seen the rubber-sheet picture: a mass dents space. But for anything slow - apples, planets, you - the term doing nearly all the work is the time–time component of the metric: near mass, time itself runs slower. Free objects follow the straightest possible path through spacetime, and when the rate of time varies from place to place, "straight" tilts toward where time runs slow.

Why uneven time turns a path

A marching column wheels toward its slow side: the marchers nearer it cover less ground, so the whole line pivots. In spacetime the "marchers" are the ticks of local clocks - a body's path bends toward the region of slow time. That bending is the pull you call gravity: g = c²·(gradient of the time rate).

Lower means slower - measurably

In 1959 Pound and Rebka caught time dilation across a 22-metre tower at Harvard. Today's optical clocks resolve the warp across one millimetre of height. Summed over 4.5 billion years, Earth's core - deeper in the well - is about 2½ years younger than its crust.

Relativity in your pocketwhy GPS must run Einstein's equations
1GPS satellite clocks sit higher in Earth's time-well, so they tick fast: +45 μs per day. 2They also orbit at 3.9 km/s, so special relativity ticks them slow: −7 μs per day. 3Net drift: +38 μs per day. Uncorrected, position fixes would slide ~10 km every day - useless by lunchtime.

The fix is applied before launch: the clocks are built to tick slightly slow on the ground, so that from orbit they tick exactly right. Every satnav fix is a test of general relativity - passed, billions of times a day.

Curved space is real - it doubles the bending of starlight - but the everyday gravity holding you in your chair is almost entirely curved time: you fall because your future points slightly downhill.
03

Newton billed mass. Einstein bills everything.

In Newton's law the only source of gravity is mass. The field equations replace it with Tμν - a 4×4 ledger of everything energy and momentum are doing at a point. Each row and column is one spacetime direction:

txyz t Tttenergy density Ttxenergy flux Ttyenergy flux Ttzenergy flux x Txtmomentum density Txxpressure Txyshear Txzshear y Tytmomentum density Tyxshear Tyypressure Tyzshear z Tztmomentum density Tzxshear Tzyshear Tzzpressure
Energy density - Newton's "mass", promoted: all energy counts, at E = mc² rates. Energy flux / momentum density - energy on the move gravitates too. (The grid is symmetric: these pair up.) Pressure - pushing outward in x, y, z. Yes: pressure itself is a source of gravity. Shear stress - sideways drags, as inside a twisting fluid or a stressed solid.
Pressure gravitates

Inside a massive neutron star, the pressure fighting collapse adds gravity, which demands more pressure, which adds more gravity… past roughly two solar masses nothing wins. That runaway is why black holes must form.

A hot thing outweighs a cold one

A wound spring, a charged battery, a box of trapped light - each weighs more than its relaxed twin by E/c². Immeasurably small in the lab, but this democracy of energy is exactly what the equation demands.

Conservation comes free

The geometry side obeys an identity: ∇·G ≡ 0, always. So the equation forces ∇·T = 0 - energy and momentum must be conserved. Matter literally cannot move in a way spacetime's bookkeeping forbids: the law of motion hides inside the field equation.

The sources of gravity are democratic: anything carrying energy or momentum bends spacetime - matter, light, pressure, even gravity's own field energy. Mass is merely the densest currency in circulation.
04

One lonely mass, and a whole zoo appears

Weeks after Einstein published - computing from an artillery post on the Russian front - Karl Schwarzschild solved the equations exactly for the empty space around a single sphere of mass M. The entire geometry hangs on one length:

The Schwarzschild radius - how small a mass must be squeezed for its escape speed to reach c
rs=2GM∕c²
Sunrs = 3.0 km Earthrs = 9 mm Yours ≈ 10⁻²⁵ m M87* black holers ≈ 120 × Earth's orbit
r = rs · the horizonThe rate of time falls to zero. Not a wall - just the surface where every future direction points inward. One-way glass, made of geometry.
r = 1.5 rs · the photon sphereLight itself orbits in circles here (unstably). This skimming light draws the bright ring in the Event Horizon Telescope images.
r = 3 rs · the ISCOThe innermost stable circular orbit. Closer in, no orbit can hold - matter spirals down. This edge sets the inner rim of accretion discs.
r ≫ rs · Newton restoredFar from the mass, orbits close into ellipses and the equation quietly hands back F = GMm/r²… almost.
…almost: Mercury's 43″

Schwarzschild orbits don't quite close - each ellipse precesses. Mercury's perihelion creeps 43 arcseconds per century beyond every known tug. When Einstein's new equations produced exactly that number, he reported heart palpitations.

Light bends double

Curved time alone gives Newton's guess for grazing starlight: 0.87″. Curved space doubles it to 1.75″. Eddington's 1919 eclipse measured the double - and made Einstein a household name overnight.

Stars have a floor

Buchdahl's theorem: no static star can hold itself smaller than 9/8 rs. Squeeze past that and no pressure in physics can win. Black holes aren't decoration - they're the equation running out of other answers.

Every landmark - horizon, photon sphere, ISCO, precession, bending - sits at a fixed multiple of rs. Learn one number and you know the whole neighborhood. The simulator two slides ahead draws all of them to scale.
05

Feed it a different Tμν, get a different universe

The field equations are a machine: describe the contents, and the machine returns the shape of spacetime. A century of physics is just different entries fed into the right-hand side:

An expanding cosmos

Fill Tμν with a uniform gas of galaxies and the equations refuse to sit still: spacetime must expand or contract (Friedmann, 1922). Einstein resisted - then Hubble measured the recession. Big Bang cosmology is this equation plus hydrogen.

Ripples in the vacuum

Set Tμν = 0 and shake: curvature travels as waves at c. A century later, LIGO heard two black holes merge 1.3 billion light-years away - a 0.2-second chirp that briefly out-shone, in wave power, all the stars in the observable universe.

Lenses made of gravity

Galaxy clusters bend passing light into arcs and Einstein rings - telescopes made of spacetime. Weighing the lensing against the visible matter is how we map dark matter.

Time-keeping tech

GPS corrects for curved time by design, and modern optical clocks sense the warp of a one-millimetre lift. Einstein's equations are now a surveying instrument.

07

Now move the mass - and spacetime rings

Everything so far has been frozen: park a mass somewhere, read off the dent. So ask the obvious next question - what if the mass moves? Newton has a strange answer: his gravity is instantaneous, so nudge the Sun and the Earth feels it in the same instant, however far away it sits. Einstein's equations forbid that flatly. Nothing outruns light - not even news about geometry. So when mass moves, the change in curvature has to travel outward at c, and a travelling ripple of curvature is a gravitational wave: a passing squeeze in the very distances between things.

Einstein's 1918 quadrupole formula - the wave a moving mass sends out
hij=2G∕c⁴r·d²Qij∕dt²h is a strain: the fractional stretch ΔL∕L of every ruler the wave passes through

Read it right to left. Q is the mass quadrupole - a number saying how far a lump of mass departs from being a perfect sphere, and along which axis. Take how fast that lopsidedness is changing (the second derivative in time), divide by the same brutal c⁴∕G stiffness that appears in the field equations, and let the result fade as 1∕r. That formula already tells you which motions are silent:

Pulse a ball - silence

Squeeze a star in and out, perfectly evenly, and its total mass never changes - a sphere is a sphere however big it is. There is no gravitational loudspeaker: the geometry outside just sits there. (Birkhoff's theorem - and the reason a pulsating star is gravitationally mute.)

Shake it sideways - still silence

Shake a mass back and forth and something has to push back - momentum is conserved, so the recoil moves the opposite way and its contribution cancels yours exactly. Radio antennas work by sloshing charge like this; gravity has no negative mass to make an unbalanced pair, so its dipole is silenced.

Spin a dumbbell - waves

What survives is the quadrupole: mass arranged so that its shape changes with time. Set the dumbbell spinning and the lopsidedness turns over twice per revolution - so the wave comes out at twice the orbital frequency. Two black holes circling each other are the loudest quadrupole the universe knows how to build.

What arrives: a stretch and a squeeze, at right angles

Float a ring of beads in space. A wave coming straight at you never pushes the beads along its own direction of travel - it works purely sideways, stretching one diameter while squeezing the one at 90°, then swapping, twice per wave cycle. Those two orientations, upright (h₊) and tilted (h×), are the only two shapes a gravitational wave has. Seen face-on down the axis of an orbit the two mix into a squeeze that rotates - the binary's spin, printed onto space itself.

The shape of the sound: chirp, then bell

Waves carry energy away, so the orbit has to pay for them: it shrinks, which makes it faster, which makes the waves louder - a runaway that ends in a collision. Pitch and volume climb together into a chirp, spike as the two horizons swallow each other, and then a single lopsided black hole shudders back into shape, humming its own bell note for a few milliseconds. Shifted into audio it sounds like a slide whistle followed by a thud.

GW150914 - three acts, 0.2 secondsthe first gravitational wave ever recorded, 14 September 2015
1Inspiral. Two black holes of 36 and 29 solar masses, 1.3 billion light-years away, spiral from under a thousand kilometres apart to touching. In LIGO's ears the pitch sweeps 35 → 250 Hz in eight cycles. 2Merger. The last orbit runs at 0.6 c. The horizons touch and fuse. Peak radiated power: 3.6 × 10⁴⁹ watts - briefly some 50× the light of every star in the observable universe. 3Ringdown. One black hole of 62 solar masses, spinning at 0.67 of its maximum, rings near 250 Hz and falls quiet in about 4 ms. Three solar masses have left the universe as waves.

36 + 29 = 62. The missing three solar masses is E = mc² spent on geometry - at its peak this pair turned mass into curvature faster than anything else we have ever observed. And that entire fortune reached Earth as a wobble of h ≈ 10⁻²¹: LIGO's 4-kilometre arms changed length by 4×10⁻¹⁸ m - a few thousandths of the width of a proton.

Why it took a century

Einstein predicted waves in 1916 and doubted anyone could ever catch one - c⁴∕G again. A strain of 10⁻²¹ across the whole Earth comes to about the width of an atomic nucleus. LIGO gets there by beating two 4 km laser arms against each other and resolving 10⁻¹⁹ m, far below the size of a proton.

The orbit pays, and it shows

You do not have to catch the wave to know it is leaving. Hulse and Taylor tracked the binary pulsar PSR B1913+16 from 1974 and found its orbit shrinking by 3.5 m per year - exactly the rate the quadrupole formula demands. Nobel Prize 1993, for waves nobody had yet heard.

The pitch names the masses

How fast the chirp rises fixes one combination of the two masses, the chirp mass ℳ = (m₁m₂)3/5∕(m₁+m₂)1/5, straight out of the quadrupole formula. Read the rising pitch and you have the masses; read the loudness and you have the distance. The waveform is the measurement.

Two black holes in a tight orbit is the one problem where every term in the field equations matters at once - curvature feeding on curvature - and no exact solution exists. It took until 2005 for supercomputers to simulate a merger without the code crashing. Next slide: run one, and watch the geometry ring.
