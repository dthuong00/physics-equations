An intuitive walk-through

The Schrödinger equation,
one step at a time

Nothing here is invented out of thin air. The equation is the ordinary energy bookkeeping of classical physics - total = kinetic + potential - rewritten for an object that is a wave rather than a point.

Follow the steps, then press play and watch that same energy budget shuffle back and forth in real time.

STEP 1

Starts from: classical energy

A rolling ball has two kinds of energy. Kinetic because it is moving, and potential because of where it sits - up a hill, inside a valley, near a charge. Add them and you get the total, which never changes as long as nothing external interferes. This one sentence is the whole seed of quantum mechanics.

Why swap ½mv2 for p2/2m? Because a wave has no well-defined velocity, but it does have a wavelength - and de Broglie's p = h/λ ties wavelength directly to momentum. Momentum is the bridge.

STEP 2

Differentiate the wave - the physics falls out

A particle is a wave, so take the simplest one there is: a plane wave, single wavelength, single frequency, marching steadily in one direction. Nothing below is postulated. We differentiate it and read off what drops out.

THE PLANE WAVE
Ψ(x, t)=Aei(kx − ωt)
k = how fast it wiggles through space  ·  ω = how fast it wiggles through time.
Two experimental facts tie those to physics: de Broglie p = ħk  and  Planck–Einstein E = ħω.
Differentiate in space → momentum → KINETIC ENERGY
1 · DIFFERENTIATE BOTH SIDES BY x
∂Ψ∂x=A(ik)ei(kx − ωt)=ikΨ
The x in the exponent drops down in front, and what is left is the original wave again. Differentiating gives you Ψ back, multiplied by ik.
2 · MULTIPLY BOTH SIDES BY −iħ
−iħ∂Ψ∂x=−iħ(ik)Ψ=ħkΨ
Because −i × i = 1, the imaginary units annihilate each other and leave a plain real number in front of Ψ.
3 · THAT NUMBER IS THE MOMENTUM
−iħ∂Ψ∂x=pΨ
de Broglie says ħk is p. So the −iħ was not a guess - it is precisely the factor that cancels the i and leaves momentum standing alone.
4 · NOW DO IT TWICE → KINETIC ENERGY
∂2Ψ∂x2=(ik)2Ψ=−k2Ψ
−ħ22m∂2Ψ∂x2=ħ2k22mΨ=p22mΨ=TΨ
Kinetic energy needs no new idea - it is momentum applied a second time, Since ħk = p, what lands in front of Ψ is exactly the classical p²/2m from Step 1.
Differentiate in time → energy
1 · DIFFERENTIATE BOTH SIDES BY t
∂Ψ∂t=A(−iω)ei(kx − ωt)=−iωΨ
Identical move on the other variable. The t term carries a minus sign in the exponent, so a − comes down with it.
2 · MULTIPLY BOTH SIDES BY +iħ
iħ∂Ψ∂t=iħ(−iω)Ψ=ħωΨ
i × (−i) = 1 this time. The prefactor here is +iħ rather than −iħ only because of that minus in the exponent.
3 · THAT NUMBER IS THE ENERGY
iħ∂Ψ∂t=EΨ
Planck–Einstein says ħω is E. This is the entire left-hand side of the Schrödinger equation - derived, not assumed.

Now the word. Every line above has the same shape: do something to Ψ → get a physical quantity back, times Ψ. The quantity was never the interesting part - the instruction on the left was. Give that instruction a name and a hat, and you have an operator:

p= −iħ∂∂x

One wave proves it for every wave

A plane wave is not a realistic particle - it stretches to infinity with the same amplitude everywhere. So why does proving things on it count?

Because every other wave is made of them. Fourier's theorem says any shape, however lumpy, can be built by adding plane waves of different k in the right proportions - Ψ = ∫ φ(k) ei(kx − ωt) dk. And differentiation is linear: the derivative of a sum is the sum of the derivatives. An operator that behaves correctly on each ingredient therefore behaves correctly on any recipe made from them. Prove it once on the simplest case, and every wave inherits it.

Now put those operators into E = T + V

Nothing new from here on. Step 2 turned a plane wave into these three instructions.

Three ordinary numbers. True for a planet, a pendulum, a ball on a hill.

EΨ = TΨ + VΨ
Read it as: the total energy carried by this wave = its kinetic share + its potential share.

Same sentence as E = T + V, only now every term is an instruction about a wave. The left side says how Ψ changes next instant; the right side says the energy content decides that change.

Total energy E - why the i?

Drop the i and you get the heat equation: everything smooths out and dies. Keep it, and the same "rate of change" instruction becomes a rotation in the complex plane instead of a decay.

A state of definite energy does not sit still - its phase spins at E/ħ radians per second while |Ψ|² stays perfectly still. Higher energy, faster spin. That is why energy and frequency are the same thing.

Kinetic T - energy is curvature

A second derivative measures how sharply a curve bends. A long, lazy wave barely bends and carries almost no kinetic energy. A tight spike bends violently and is expensive.

This is why atoms don't collapse. squeezing an electron toward the nucleus lowers V but the forced curvature drives T up faster.

The same rule says a trapped wave can never stop bending, so its kinetic energy can never reach zero, even at absolute zero.

Potential V - the shape of the problem

Notice it only multiplies. It never differentiates, never mixes neighbouring points. At each location it just says "energy here costs this much", and the wave responds by choosing where it can live.

Everything you actually model lives in this one term ---- a Coulomb well for hydrogen (-ke^2/r), a parabola for a vibrating bond, a rectangle for a quantum dot. You supply V; that is the problem, and the equation does the rest.

Try: the quantum pendulum

Harmonic well, k₀ = 0. The packet slides down, speeds up, climbs the far side and comes back - like a ball in a bowl. Green and orange swap; blue never moves.

Try: tunnelling

Barrier, k₀ ≈ 2.5, strength ≈ 1. Classically it must bounce. Quantum-mechanically a sliver leaks through. That leak is why the sun shines.

Try: spreading

Free particle, σ = 0.5. A pinched packet needs sharp curvature, sharp curvature means large kinetic energy, and it flies apart. σ = 3 barely spreads.
